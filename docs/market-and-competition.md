# Market and competition

## Who we are building for

Our main users are small farmers, starting with rice growers, who have a basic or mid-range Android phone, are comfortable speaking Bangla and less comfortable with apps full of menus. Around them are the people who already help farmers: agro-input dealers, NGO field workers and extension staff. Those groups are also our likely customers.

## What farmers can use today

Farmers in Bangladesh are not starting from zero. These are the tools we found and studied.

- **Dr. Chashi** uses AI to detect crop diseases and, according to a [TBS report](https://www.tbsnews.net/node/630290), also offers a voice assistant and a call centre.
- **BRRI's rice app (ধান সুরক্ষা)** comes from the national rice research institute and gives rice farmers solutions to common problems. It is on [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.brriapps).
- **Crops Doctor** is an AI assistant for farmers built by a student at HSTU ([BSS](https://www.bssnews.net/news/411029/print)).
- **Krishi Sheba** from Grameenphone is a farming information service run by a mobile operator. A [GSMA case study](https://www.gsma.com/mobilefordevelopment/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/gp-krishi-sheba-mobile-agriculture-service-grameenphone-bangladesh.pdf) says it was meant to improve farmers' access to information and also to build customer loyalty.
- **Krishi Desk** focuses on market prices and bookings ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=ss.krishidesk)).
- **Digital Khamari** is a livestock app that can be used offline ([UNB](https://unb.com.bd/news/tag/143843)).
- **KrishokBondhu** is a research project on a voice-based advice call centre for Bengali farmers ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18355)).

## What this tells us

Photo-based disease detection is already out there, and so are some voice features. We do not think either one on its own is a reason to build a new app, so we are not building around them.

What we noticed is that most tools give an answer from a photo or a menu choice, and then stop. A real adviser asks questions first, because the same yellow leaf can come from a disease, a pest, too much water or too little fertilizer. Most tools also depend on a good connection and expect the farmer to read.

## Where KrishiSathi fits

- It holds a short spoken conversation, asking a few questions before it answers.
- It answers from published guidance and says plainly when it isn't sure.
- It stays with the farmer through the season, with a field profile, stage-based reminders and illustrated guides with audio.
- The guides, reminders and records work offline.
- Everything is in Bangla, and every important screen can be heard as well as read.

Rice is our starting crop because it is the main one and the public guidance for it is strong. The product is not tied to it: what the assistant can answer is decided by the guidance it has been given, so a new crop is new content, not a new app.

Competitors can copy features. What is harder to copy is the quality of the advice and the relationships with the organisations that work with farmers, so that is where we plan to put our effort.

## Why now

AI models can now understand spoken Bangla and photos reasonably well, which was not practical a few years ago. Combined with farmers who already carry smartphones, this makes a spoken adviser realistic. We are testing how well it works with real Bangla recordings and leaf photos before we make any claims about accuracy.
